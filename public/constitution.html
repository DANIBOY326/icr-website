Charter · 2026

Preamble

We, the undergraduate students, postgraduate scholars, early-career academics, and research enthusiasts united under the banner of Innovative Campus Researchers (ICR), recognizing the transformative power of ethical inquiry, interdisciplinary collaboration, and applied learning across tertiary institutions, do hereby establish and adopt this Constitution to govern our non-profit academic community with transparency, rigor, and shared accountability.

Article I — Name, Status, and Motto

  1. 1.1 Name: The organization shall be officially known as Innovative Campus Researchers, abbreviated as ICR.
  2. 1.2 Non-Profit Status: ICR is a strictly non-profit, non-partisan, and academic community dedicated to student and early-career scholarly advancement.
  3. 1.3 Motto: The guiding motto of the community shall be "Research · Innovate · Think."

Article II — Vision, Mission, and Objectives

  1. 2.1 Vision: To become a leading campus-based research community that produces innovative, ethical, and globally competitive researchers.
  2. 2.2 Mission: To build the next generation of researchers through structured collaboration, applied learning, mentorship, and real-world research engagement.
  3. 2.3 Core Objectives:
    • Facilitate collaborative cross-campus research teams and peer-review circles.
    • Organize capacity-building methodology clinics, academic writing workshops, and flagship summits.
    • Maintain an open-access Working Paper Archive for student and early-career publications.
    • Promote responsible, transparent use of emerging research technologies including Artificial Intelligence.

Article III — Membership, Rights, and Obligations

  1. 3.1 Eligibility: Membership is open to undergraduate students, postgraduate scholars, early-career academics, and research enthusiasts across tertiary institutions who subscribe to ICR's Core Values.
  2. 3.2 Rights of Members: Every active member holds the right to participate in research clinics, submit manuscripts for peer review, access shared community resources, attend general assemblies, and vote in executive elections.
  3. 3.3 Obligations of Members: Members must uphold scholarly honesty, respect peer reviewers and collaborators, avoid plagiarism or data fabrication, and contribute constructively to the community.

Article IV — Governance & Executive Council

  1. 4.1 Executive Structure: Day-to-day stewardship of ICR is vested in the Executive Council comprising the President, Vice President, Director of Research & Publications, Secretary General, Programs & Events Coordinator, and Editorial & Communications Lead.
  2. 4.2 Tenure & Handover: Executive officers serve a structured academic term with a mandatory documented handover of archives, records, and ongoing research cohorts to incoming leadership.
  3. 4.3 Peer-Review Committee: The Director of Research chairs an internal Peer-Review Committee responsible for evaluating manuscripts submitted to the ICR Working Paper Archive.

Article V — Meetings, Assemblies, and Quorum

  1. 5.1 General Assembly: The General Assembly convenes at least once per academic semester to review community milestones, approve major initiatives, and elect officers.
  2. 5.2 Research Clinics: Academic clinics and peer-review sessions convene monthly in virtual or hybrid formats open to all active members.
  3. 5.3 Quorum: A simple majority of Executive Council members (or one-third of active registered members for General Assemblies) constitutes a valid quorum for binding decisions.

Article VI — Academic Integrity, Ethics, and Finance

  1. 6.1 Zero Tolerance for Misconduct: Plagiarism, fabricated datasets, undisclosed authorship, and uncredited copying violate ICR's foundational charter and warrant manuscript rejection or disciplinary review.
  2. 6.2 Responsible AI Standard: Members utilizing AI assistants for editing, literature triage, or coding must verify every citation independently and disclose tool usage where applicable.
  3. 6.3 Financial Transparency: Any grants, sponsorships, or voluntary program funds shall be applied exclusively toward educational programs, open publishing, and member capacity building, with transparent annual reporting.

Article VII — Constitutional Amendments

  1. 7.1 Proposal: Amendments may be proposed by a two-thirds resolution of the Executive Council or by written petition signed by at least twenty percent (20%) of active members.
  2. 7.2 Ratification: Proposed amendments shall be circulated to all members at least fourteen (14) days prior to voting and ratified by a two-thirds majority of votes cast at a General Assembly.
